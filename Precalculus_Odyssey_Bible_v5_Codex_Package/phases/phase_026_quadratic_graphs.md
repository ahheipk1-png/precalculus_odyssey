# Phase 026 - Quadratic graphs

## Phase Metadata
- Course: Precalculus Odyssey Bible v5
- Topic: Quadratic graphs
- Subtopic: Reading, sketching, and interpreting parabolas from multiple forms
- Prerequisites: quadratic factoring, completing the square, quadratic formula, coordinate graphing
- Related phases: Phase 025 review; Phase 027 follow-up; mixed review and final boss integration
- Estimated phase size: 20 template families, 20 core examples, 120 progressive hints, 1 tutorial sequence, 1 Socratic dialogue system

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this phase, the player can:

1. Recognize the structure of quadratic graphs tasks before choosing a method.
2. Use correct notation, restrictions, and units while solving quadratic graphs questions.
3. Solve tasks involving read vertex form.
4. Solve tasks involving axis of symmetry.
5. Solve tasks involving opening direction.
6. Solve tasks involving width comparison.
7. Solve tasks involving y-intercept from standard form.
8. Solve tasks involving x-intercepts from factored form.
9. Solve tasks involving vertex from factored form.
10. Solve tasks involving vertex by completing square.

## Prerequisite Review
- A quadratic graph is a parabola.
- Vertex form y=a(x-h)^2+k shows vertex (h,k).
- Factored form y=a(x-r1)(x-r2) shows x-intercepts.
- Standard form y=ax^2+bx+c shows the y-intercept c.
- The sign of a decides whether the graph opens up or down.

## Core Concepts
- The vertex is the turning point of a parabola.
- The axis of symmetry is the vertical line through the vertex.
- The sign and size of a control opening direction and width.
- x-intercepts are real zeros of the quadratic.
- The y-intercept occurs when x=0.
- A quadratic graph can be described using vertex, intercepts, and opening direction.

## Common Misconceptions
- Reading the vertex sign backward from vertex form.
- Confusing x-intercepts with the y-intercept.
- Forgetting that a negative leading coefficient opens down.
- Using all real numbers as a context domain when time or length is restricted.
- Assuming the graph scale does not affect interpretation.

## Core Concept
- The vertex is the turning point of a parabola.
- The axis of symmetry is the vertical line through the vertex.
- The sign and size of a control opening direction and width.
- x-intercepts are real zeros of the quadratic.
- The y-intercept occurs when x=0.
- A quadratic graph can be described using vertex, intercepts, and opening direction.

## Worked Example
- For y=2(x-3)^2-5, identify the vertex. Answer: (3,-5) Reason: Vertex form y=a(x-h)^2+k gives vertex (h,k), so h=3 and k=-5.
- Find the axis of symmetry of y=(x+4)^2+1. Answer: x=-4 Reason: The vertex is (-4,1), so the axis is x=-4.
- Does y=-3(x-1)^2+7 open up or down? Answer: down Reason: The coefficient a=-3 is negative, so the parabola opens down.
- Which is narrower: y=x^2 or y=4x^2? Answer: y=4x^2 Reason: A larger absolute value of a makes the parabola vertically steeper and narrower.

## Common Mistakes
- Reading the vertex sign backward from vertex form.
- Confusing x-intercepts with the y-intercept.
- Forgetting that a negative leading coefficient opens down.
- Using all real numbers as a context domain when time or length is restricted.
- Assuming the graph scale does not affect interpretation.
