# Phase 030 - Polynomial division

## Phase Metadata
- Course: Precalculus Odyssey Bible v5

- Subtopic: Dividing polynomials using long division and synthetic division
- Prerequisites: polynomial operations, factoring, exponents, linear divisors
- Related phases: Phase 029 review; Phase 031 follow-up; mixed review and final boss integration

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this phase, the player can:

1. Recognize the structure of polynomial division tasks before choosing a method.
2. Use correct notation, restrictions, and units while solving polynomial division questions.
3. Solve tasks involving simple synthetic division.
4. Solve tasks involving synthetic with remainder.
5. Solve tasks involving c value for divisor.
6. Solve tasks involving divisor x+7.
7. Solve tasks involving cubic by linear.
8. Solve tasks involving missing term coefficient.
9. Solve tasks involving long division first step.
10. Solve tasks involving long division binomial.

## Prerequisite Review
- A polynomial is a sum of powers of x with real coefficients.
- The degree is the highest exponent with a nonzero coefficient.
- Zeros are x-values where the polynomial equals zero.
- Factored form shows zeros and multiplicities.
- Polynomial division rewrites one polynomial as quotient plus remainder over divisor.

## Core Concepts
- Polynomial division rewrites dividend divided by divisor as quotient plus remainder over divisor.
- Long division works for any polynomial divisor.
- Synthetic division is a shortcut for divisors of the form x-c.
- Missing powers need zero coefficient entries.
- The remainder has degree lower than the divisor.
- A zero remainder means the divisor is a factor.

## Common Misconceptions
- Using the constant term to decide end behavior.
- Counting a repeated factor only once when multiplicity matters.
- Dropping zero coefficient entries during polynomial division.
- Confusing a remainder value with an x-intercept.
- Forgetting to test the original polynomial after factoring.

## Why It Matters
This topic appears in later modeling, graphing, and boss challenges because it connects symbolic work with decisions about structure, restrictions, and meaning.

## Prerequisite Check
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? A polynomial is a sum of powers of x with real coefficients.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? The degree is the highest exponent with a nonzero coefficient.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Zeros are x-values where the polynomial equals zero.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Factored form shows zeros and multiplicities.

## Core Concept
- Polynomial division rewrites dividend divided by divisor as quotient plus remainder over divisor.
- Long division works for any polynomial divisor.
- Synthetic division is a shortcut for divisors of the form x-c.
- Missing powers need zero coefficient entries.
- The remainder has degree lower than the divisor.
- A zero remainder means the divisor is a factor.

## Worked Example
- Divide x^2+5x+6 by x+2. Answer: x+3 remainder 0 Reason: Use c=-2. Synthetic division gives quotient x+3 and remainder 0.
- Divide x^2+3x+5 by x+1. Answer: x+2 remainder 3 Reason: Use c=-1. The quotient is x+2 and the remainder is 3.
- For division by x-4, what number is used in synthetic division? Answer: 4 Reason: The divisor x-c uses c=4.
- For division by x+7, what number is used in synthetic division? Answer: -7 Reason: x+7=x-(-7), so c=-7.

## Common Mistakes
- Using the constant term to decide end behavior.
- Counting a repeated factor only once when multiplicity matters.
- Dropping zero coefficient entries during polynomial division.
- Confusing a remainder value with an x-intercept.
- Forgetting to test the original polynomial after factoring.

Player response is classified by method choice, accuracy, notation, and interpretation.

## Guided Discovery
Tutor asks the player to identify the known information, the target, the rule or representation, and any restrictions before computing.
The sequence moves from recognition to one decisive step, then to a final interpretation.

## Correct Branch
If the player chooses the right structure, the tutor asks for the computation and then a sentence explaining why the result is allowed.

## Partial Understanding Branch
If the player has the right idea but incomplete execution, the tutor keeps their setup and asks for the next legal move.

## Misconception Branch
- If the player shows this issue: Using the constant term to decide end behavior. The tutor asks for a counterexample from the worked examples.
- If the player shows this issue: Counting a repeated factor only once when multiplicity matters. The tutor asks for a counterexample from the worked examples.
- If the player shows this issue: Dropping zero coefficient entries during polynomial division. The tutor asks for a counterexample from the worked examples.
- If the player shows this issue: Confusing a remainder value with an x-intercept. The tutor asks for a counterexample from the worked examples.

## Unsure Branch
Tutor: "Point to the part of the problem that tells us which rule, formula, graph feature, or restriction controls the answer."
Then the tutor offers two choices and asks the player to justify one.

## Unrelated Response Branch
Tutor restates the smallest actionable question and asks the player to choose between the two most plausible first moves.

## Recovery Prompts
- What is being asked: value, equation, graph feature, interpretation, or construction?
- Which rule or condition applies first?
- Are there restrictions on inputs, outputs, units, or angles?
- Does the final answer satisfy the original problem?
- Can you explain the answer in one sentence?

## Reflection Question
Why is method selection more important than memorizing one procedure for every polynomial division task?

## Transfer Question
Where could polynomial division appear inside a mixed review or final boss challenge?

## Escalation Rules
- If the same misconception repeats twice, return to the relevant worked example.
- If the player cannot start, show the prerequisite review first.
- If the player solves three guided items correctly, move to independent practice.
- If the player solves two boss-compatible items correctly, unlock mixed review.

## Exit Condition
The Socratic sequence is complete when the player can choose the method, compute accurately, respect restrictions, and explain the result without prompting.
