# Phase 057 - Complex numbers

## Phase Metadata
- Course: Precalculus Odyssey Bible v5
- Topic: Complex numbers
- Subtopic: Operations, conjugates, quadratic roots, modulus, and polar form
- Prerequisites: integer arithmetic, radicals, quadratic formula, trigonometric exact values
- Related phases: Phase 056 review; Phase 058 follow-up; mixed review and final boss integration
- Estimated phase size: 20 template families, 20 core examples, 120 progressive hints, 1 tutorial sequence, 1 Socratic dialogue system

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this phase, the player can:

1. Recognize the structure of complex numbers tasks before choosing a method.
2. Use correct notation, restrictions, and units while solving complex numbers questions.
3. Solve tasks involving simplifying powers of i.
4. Solve tasks involving adding complex numbers.
5. Solve tasks involving subtracting complex numbers.
6. Solve tasks involving multiplying complex numbers.
7. Solve tasks involving using conjugate products.
8. Solve tasks involving dividing complex numbers.
9. Solve tasks involving solving a pure imaginary quadratic.
10. Solve tasks involving using the quadratic formula with complex roots.

## Prerequisite Review
- Use i^2 = -1 whenever powers or products of i appear.
- Combine real parts with real parts and imaginary parts with imaginary parts.
- Use the quadratic formula when real factoring no longer works.
- Use the Pythagorean theorem to find modulus in the complex plane.
- Use exact trig values to convert between rectangular and polar forms.

## Core Concepts
- A complex number a + bi has real part a and imaginary part b.
- The conjugate a - bi helps divide complex numbers and creates real products.
- Complex roots naturally appear when a quadratic has a negative discriminant.
- The modulus of a + bi is its distance from the origin in the complex plane.
- Polar form uses radius and angle, so multiplication scales radii and adds angles.

## Common Misconceptions
- Treating i^2 as positive 1 instead of negative 1.
- Combining real and imaginary terms as if they were like terms.
- Dividing complex numbers without multiplying by the conjugate.
- Forgetting that polar angles need the correct quadrant.
- Keeping both plus and minus roots when a context requires a positive real input.

## Learning Goal
Master complex numbers by choosing the right representation, completing the computation, and interpreting the result.

## Why It Matters

## Prerequisite Check

- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Use the quadratic formula when real factoring no longer works.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Use the Pythagorean theorem to find modulus in the complex plane.

## Core Concept
- A complex number a + bi has real part a and imaginary part b.
- The conjugate a - bi helps divide complex numbers and creates real products.
- Complex roots naturally appear when a quadratic has a negative discriminant.
- The modulus of a + bi is its distance from the origin in the complex plane.
- Polar form uses radius and angle, so multiplication scales radii and adds angles.

## Worked Example
- Simplify i^37. Answer: i. Reason: Powers of i cycle every 4: i, -1, -i, 1. Since 37 leaves remainder 1 when divided by 4, i^37 = i.
- Compute (3 + 2i) + (5 - 7i). Answer: 8 - 5i. Reason: Add real parts and imaginary parts separately: 3 + 5 = 8 and 2i - 7i = -5i.
- Compute (4 - 3i) - (1 + 6i). Answer: 3 - 9i. Reason: Distribute the subtraction: 4 - 3i - 1 - 6i = 3 - 9i.
- Compute (2 + 3i)(4 - i). Answer: 11 + 10i. Reason: Multiply: 8 - 2i + 12i - 3i^2. Since i^2 = -1, -3i^2 = 3, so the result is 11 + 10i.

## Common Mistakes
- Treating i^2 as positive 1 instead of negative 1.
- Combining real and imaginary terms as if they were like terms.
- Dividing complex numbers without multiplying by the conjugate.
- Forgetting that polar angles need the correct quadrant.
- Keeping both plus and minus roots when a context requires a positive real input.
