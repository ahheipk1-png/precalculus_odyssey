## Prerequisite Review
- Logarithms convert multiplication into addition.
- Logarithms convert division into subtraction.
- Exponents inside a logarithm can move to the front as coefficients.
- Arguments of all logarithms must be positive.
- Change of base rewrites a logarithm as a quotient of logs with a shared base.

## Core Concepts
- Product property: log_b(MN) = log_b(M) + log_b(N).
- Quotient property: log_b(M/N) = log_b(M) - log_b(N).
- Power property: log_b(M^p) = p log_b(M).
- Condensing reverses expansion and requires matching bases.
- Change of base: log_b(a) = log(a)/log(b) = ln(a)/ln(b).
- Expanded forms can introduce hidden domain conditions from each log argument.

## Common Misconceptions
- Writing log(a + b) as log(a) + log(b).
- Moving a coefficient into the log as multiplication instead of as a power.
- Condensing logs with different bases.
- Forgetting parentheses when applying quotient properties.
- Ignoring positive-argument restrictions after expanding.

# Part III - Tutorial Bible

## Learning Goal
Master logarithm properties by choosing the right representation, completing the computation, and interpreting the result.

## Why It Matters
This topic appears in later modeling, graphing, and boss challenges because it connects symbolic work with decisions about structure, restrictions, and meaning.

## Prerequisite Check
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Logarithms convert multiplication into addition.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Logarithms convert division into subtraction.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Exponents inside a logarithm can move to the front as coefficients.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Arguments of all logarithms must be positive.

## Core Concept
- Product property: log_b(MN) = log_b(M) + log_b(N).
- Quotient property: log_b(M/N) = log_b(M) - log_b(N).
- Power property: log_b(M^p) = p log_b(M).
- Condensing reverses expansion and requires matching bases.
- Change of base: log_b(a) = log(a)/log(b) = ln(a)/ln(b).
- Expanded forms can introduce hidden domain conditions from each log argument.

## Worked Example
- Expand log_3(5x). Answer: log_3(5) + log_3(x) Reason: The product property changes multiplication inside one log into addition of two logs.
- Expand log_2(x/7). Answer: log_2(x) - log_2(7) Reason: The quotient property changes division inside the log into subtraction.
- Expand log_5(y^4). Answer: 4log_5(y) Reason: The power property moves the exponent 4 to the front as a coefficient.

## Common Mistakes

- Moving a coefficient into the log as multiplication instead of as a power.
- Condensing logs with different bases.
- Forgetting parentheses when applying quotient properties.
- Ignoring positive-argument restrictions after expanding.
