10. Solve tasks involving interpret y-intercept.

## Prerequisite Review
- A quadratic graph is a parabola.
- Vertex form y=a(x-h)^2+k shows vertex (h,k).
- Factored form y=a(x-r1)(x-r2) shows x-intercepts.
- Standard form y=ax^2+bx+c shows the y-intercept c.
- The sign of a decides whether the graph opens up or down.

## Core Concepts
- Quadratic models often describe area, height, revenue, and symmetrical optimization.
- The vertex often represents a maximum or minimum in context.
- Zeros can represent times, break-even values, or dimensions where output is zero.
- The domain must fit the situation, not just the algebra.
- Units should be included in interpreted answers.
- Model parameters can be found from points or constraints.

## Common Misconceptions
- Reading the vertex sign backward from vertex form.
- Confusing x-intercepts with the y-intercept.
- Forgetting that a negative leading coefficient opens down.
- Using all real numbers as a context domain when time or length is restricted.
- Assuming the graph scale does not affect interpretation.

- Units should be included in interpreted answers.
- Model parameters can be found from points or constraints.

- Confusing x-intercepts with the y-intercept.
- Forgetting that a negative leading coefficient opens down.
- Using all real numbers as a context domain when time or length is restricted.
- Assuming the graph scale does not affect interpretation.
