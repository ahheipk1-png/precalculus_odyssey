# Phase 023 - Completing the square

## Phase Metadata
- Course: Precalculus Odyssey Bible v5
- Topic: Completing the square
- Subtopic: Rewriting quadratics in vertex form and solving by completing the square
- Prerequisites: quadratic expressions, factoring perfect squares, square roots, equation balancing
- Related phases: Phase 022 review; Phase 024 follow-up; mixed review and final boss integration
- Estimated phase size: 20 template families, 20 core examples, 120 progressive hints, 1 tutorial sequence, 1 Socratic dialogue system

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this phase, the player can:

1. Recognize the structure of completing the square tasks before choosing a method.
2. Use correct notation, restrictions, and units while solving completing the square questions.
3. Solve tasks involving find completing value positive b.
4. Solve tasks involving find completing value negative b.
5. Solve tasks involving rewrite as square.
6. Solve tasks involving complete expression.
7. Solve tasks involving vertex form a=1.
8. Solve tasks involving read vertex.
9. Solve tasks involving solve simple complete square.
10. Solve tasks involving solve nonfactorable.

## Prerequisite Review
- A perfect square trinomial has the form x^2+2kx+k^2.
- Half of the x-coefficient, squared, completes x^2+bx.
- Equations stay balanced only when the same value is added to both sides.
- Vertex form is a(x-h)^2+k.
- Square roots produce positive and negative solutions when solving equations.

## Core Concepts
- Complete the square by adding (b/2)^2 to x^2+bx.
- A completed square reveals the vertex of a parabola.
- For equations, move constants first, complete the square, then take square roots.
- When a is not 1, factor a from the quadratic and linear terms first.
- Completing the square works even when a quadratic is not factorable.
- The process connects algebraic form to graph features.

## Common Misconceptions
- Squaring b instead of half of b.
- Adding a value inside an equation without balancing the other side.
- Taking only the positive square root.
- Forgetting to factor the leading coefficient before completing the square.
- Reading the vertex sign backward in (x-h)^2+k.

# Part III - Tutorial Bible

## Learning Goal
Master completing the square by choosing the right representation, completing the computation, and interpreting the result.

## Why It Matters
This topic appears in later modeling, graphing, and boss challenges because it connects symbolic work with decisions about structure, restrictions, and meaning.

## Prerequisite Check
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? A perfect square trinomial has the form x^2+2kx+k^2.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Half of the x-coefficient, squared, completes x^2+bx.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Equations stay balanced only when the same value is added to both sides.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Vertex form is a(x-h)^2+k.

## Core Concept
- Complete the square by adding (b/2)^2 to x^2+bx.
- A completed square reveals the vertex of a parabola.
- For equations, move constants first, complete the square, then take square roots.
- When a is not 1, factor a from the quadratic and linear terms first.
- Completing the square works even when a quadratic is not factorable.
- The process connects algebraic form to graph features.

## Worked Example
- What number completes the square for x^2+8x? Answer: 16 Reason: Half of 8 is 4, and 4^2=16.
- What number completes the square for x^2-6x? Answer: 9 Reason: Half of -6 is -3, and (-3)^2=9.
- Rewrite x^2+10x+25 as a squared binomial. Answer: (x+5)^2 Reason: Half of 10 is 5, so the trinomial is (x+5)^2.
- Complete the square: x^2+4x+7. Answer: (x+2)^2+3 Reason: Add and subtract 4: x^2+4x+4+3=(x+2)^2+3.

## Common Mistakes
- Squaring b instead of half of b.
- Adding a value inside an equation without balancing the other side.
- Taking only the positive square root.
- Forgetting to factor the leading coefficient before completing the square.
- Reading the vertex sign backward in (x-h)^2+k.
