## Prerequisite Review
- On the unit circle, a point has coordinates (cos theta, sin theta).
- Quadrants determine the signs of sine and cosine.
- The special reference angles are 30 degrees, 45 degrees, and 60 degrees, or pi/6, pi/4, and pi/3.
- Tangent equals sin theta divided by cos theta when cos theta is not zero.
- Coterminal angles share the same unit-circle point.

## Core Concepts
- The unit circle has radius 1 and center at the origin.
- Cosine is the x-coordinate and sine is the y-coordinate.
- Reference angles give the absolute values of special trig coordinates.
- Quadrant signs turn reference values into exact unit-circle values.
- Quadrantal angles have coordinates on the axes.
- Solving simple trig equations on 0 to 2pi means finding all matching unit-circle angles.

## Common Misconceptions
- Swapping sine and cosine coordinates.
- Using the right reference values but the wrong quadrant signs.
- Giving only one angle when two unit-circle angles have the same sine or cosine.
- Treating tangent as defined when cosine is zero.
- Mixing degree and radian labels in the same answer.

## Core Concept
- The unit circle has radius 1 and center at the origin.
- Cosine is the x-coordinate and sine is the y-coordinate.
- Reference angles give the absolute values of special trig coordinates.
- Quadrant signs turn reference values into exact unit-circle values.
- Quadrantal angles have coordinates on the axes.
- Solving simple trig equations on 0 to 2pi means finding all matching unit-circle angles.

## Worked Example
- What point on the unit circle corresponds to theta = 0? Answer: (1, 0) Reason: At angle 0, the terminal side points along the positive x-axis, so the point is (1, 0).
- Give the unit-circle coordinates for theta = pi/6. Answer: (sqrt(3)/2, 1/2) Reason: The reference angle is 30 degrees in Quadrant I, so cosine is sqrt(3)/2 and sine is 1/2.
- Give the unit-circle coordinates for theta = pi/4. Answer: (sqrt(2)/2, sqrt(2)/2) Reason: A 45 degree reference angle has equal positive coordinates on the unit circle.
- Give the unit-circle coordinates for theta = pi/3. Answer: (1/2, sqrt(3)/2) Reason: The reference angle is 60 degrees in Quadrant I, so cosine is 1/2 and sine is sqrt(3)/2.

## Common Mistakes
- Swapping sine and cosine coordinates.
- Using the right reference values but the wrong quadrant signs.
- Giving only one angle when two unit-circle angles have the same sine or cosine.
- Treating tangent as defined when cosine is zero.
- Mixing degree and radian labels in the same answer.
