## Prerequisite Review
- A full circle is 360 degrees or 2pi radians.
- A half circle is 180 degrees or pi radians.
- To convert degrees to radians, multiply by pi/180.
- To convert radians to degrees, multiply by 180/pi.
- Arc length uses s = r theta when theta is measured in radians.

## Core Concepts
- Degrees and radians are two units for measuring the same angle rotation.
- Radians connect angle measure to arc length on a circle.
- Coterminal angles differ by full rotations of 360 degrees or 2pi radians.
- Positive angles rotate counterclockwise; negative angles rotate clockwise.
- Special angles often convert to simple multiples of pi.
- Angular speed can be measured in radians per unit time.

## Common Misconceptions
- Multiplying by 180/pi when converting degrees to radians.
- Leaving degree symbols on radian answers or pi on degree answers.
- Using s = r theta while theta is still in degrees.
- Adding 180 degrees instead of 360 degrees for coterminal angles.
- Treating pi radians as 90 degrees instead of 180 degrees.

# Part III - Tutorial Bible

## Learning Goal
Master degree and radian measure by choosing the right representation, completing the computation, and interpreting the result.

## Why It Matters
This topic appears in later modeling, graphing, and boss challenges because it connects symbolic work with decisions about structure, restrictions, and meaning.

## Prerequisite Check
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? A full circle is 360 degrees or 2pi radians.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? A half circle is 180 degrees or pi radians.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? To convert degrees to radians, multiply by pi/180.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? To convert radians to degrees, multiply by 180/pi.

## Core Concept
- Degrees and radians are two units for measuring the same angle rotation.
- Radians connect angle measure to arc length on a circle.
- Coterminal angles differ by full rotations of 360 degrees or 2pi radians.
- Positive angles rotate counterclockwise; negative angles rotate clockwise.
- Special angles often convert to simple multiples of pi.
- Angular speed can be measured in radians per unit time.

## Worked Example
- Convert 60 degrees to radians. Answer: pi/3 radians Reason: Multiply by pi/180: 60(pi/180) = pi/3.
- Convert 3pi/4 radians to degrees. Answer: 135 degrees Reason: Multiply by 180/pi: (3pi/4)(180/pi) = 135 degrees.
- Convert -45 degrees to radians. Answer: -pi/4 radians Reason: Multiply by pi/180: -45(pi/180) = -pi/4.
- Convert -5pi/6 radians to degrees. Answer: -150 degrees Reason: Multiply by 180/pi: (-5pi/6)(180/pi) = -150 degrees.

## Common Mistakes
- Multiplying by 180/pi when converting degrees to radians.
- Leaving degree symbols on radian answers or pi on degree answers.
- Using s = r theta while theta is still in degrees.
- Adding 180 degrees instead of 360 degrees for coterminal angles.
- Treating pi radians as 90 degrees instead of 180 degrees.
