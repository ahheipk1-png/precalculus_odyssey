# Phase 054 - Series

## Phase Metadata
- Course: Precalculus Odyssey Bible v5
- Topic: Series
- Subtopic: Finite arithmetic and geometric sums, infinite geometric series, and sigma notation
- Prerequisites: Sequences, arithmetic rules, geometric rules, exponents, fractions, and summation notation
- Related phases: Phase 053 review; Phase 055 follow-up; mixed review and final boss integration
- Estimated phase size: 20 template families, 20 core examples, 120 progressive hints, 1 tutorial sequence, 1 Socratic dialogue system

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this phase, the player can:

1. Recognize the structure of series tasks before choosing a method.
2. Use correct notation, restrictions, and units while solving series questions.
3. Solve tasks involving adding a short arithmetic series.
4. Solve tasks involving arithmetic series formula.
5. Solve tasks involving finite arithmetic sum.
6. Solve tasks involving adding a finite geometric series.
7. Solve tasks involving finite geometric series formula.
8. Solve tasks involving finite geometric sum.
9. Solve tasks involving infinite geometric sum.
10. Solve tasks involving infinite series from listed terms.

## Prerequisite Review
- Find terms of arithmetic and geometric sequences.
- Distinguish a term a_n from a sum S_n.
- Evaluate powers and fractions accurately.
- Interpret sigma notation by expanding the indicated terms.

## Core Concepts
- A series is the sum of the terms of a sequence.
- A finite arithmetic series can be summed with S_n = n(a_1 + a_n)/2.
- A finite geometric series can be summed with S_n = a_1(1 - r^n)/(1 - r), when r is not 1.
- An infinite geometric series has sum a_1/(1 - r) only when |r| < 1.
- Sigma notation names an index, lower bound, upper bound, and expression to sum.

## Common Misconceptions
- Finding the nth term when the question asks for the sum.
- Using the infinite geometric formula when |r| is greater than or equal to 1.
- Starting a sigma sum at the wrong index.
- Forgetting that alternating geometric series can have negative ratios.
- Mixing arithmetic and geometric sum formulas.

# Part III - Tutorial Bible

## Learning Goal

## Why It Matters

## Prerequisite Check

- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Distinguish a term a_n from a sum S_n.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Evaluate powers and fractions accurately.
- Can the player explain this prerequisite? Interpret sigma notation by expanding the indicated terms.

## Core Concept
- A series is the sum of the terms of a sequence.
- A finite arithmetic series can be summed with S_n = n(a_1 + a_n)/2.
- A finite geometric series can be summed with S_n = a_1(1 - r^n)/(1 - r), when r is not 1.
- An infinite geometric series has sum a_1/(1 - r) only when |r| < 1.
- Sigma notation names an index, lower bound, upper bound, and expression to sum.

## Worked Example
- Find the sum 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10. Answer: 30 Reason: Add the five terms: 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10 = 30. This is also an arithmetic series with average term 6 and 5 terms, so the sum is 5(6) = 30.
- What formula gives the sum of the first n terms of an arithmetic series when a_1 and a_n are known? Answer: S_n = n(a_1 + a_n)/2 Reason: The sum equals the number of terms times the average of the first and last terms. The average is (a_1 + a_n)/2.
- Find the sum of the first 10 terms of the arithmetic sequence with a_1 = 3 and d = 2. Answer: 120 Reason: First find a_10 = 3 + 2(9) = 21. Then S_10 = 10(3 + 21)/2 = 120.
- Find the sum 3 + 6 + 12 + 24. Answer: 45 Reason: Add the terms directly: 3 + 6 + 12 + 24 = 45. The common ratio is 2, but direct addition is quick for four terms.

## Common Mistakes
- Finding the nth term when the question asks for the sum.
- Using the infinite geometric formula when |r| is greater than or equal to 1.
- Starting a sigma sum at the wrong index.
- Forgetting that alternating geometric series can have negative ratios.
- Mixing arithmetic and geometric sum formulas.
